Birth Injuries

Birth Injuries May Require a Lifetime of Medical Care

Cerebral Palsy is a type of brain injury, often caused by decreased oxygen and/or blood flow to the baby, resulting in a neurologic disorder that can lead to significant physical and mental disabilities for the child.

Errors during childbirth can also lead to other serious conditions, such as hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), brain injury or brain damage, developmental delays, intellectual disability, mental problems, autism, and seizure disorders. In many cases, these serious disorders could have been prevented with appropriate medical care during pregnancy, labor, and delivery.

All of these conditions can require expensive care and treatment to maximize the child's quality of life and their chances for improvement. These can include:

  • Long-term residential care and in-home supervision
  • Therapies and assistive devices
  • Medications and medical specialists
  • Home modifications and surgeries

To provide the best future possible for their children and to relieve this financial burden, families will often pursue compensation from the responsible parties.

How It Happens

You Might Be Entitled to Compensation

While there are some instances in which a birth injury occurs for no obvious reason and no one is to blame, a serious diagnosis is often preceded by events like these — among other things:

Emergency Cesarean Section

An emergency C-section was needed during labor or delivery.

Prematurity & Preterm Labor

Premature birth, preterm labor, cervical insufficiency, or a short cervix.

NICU Stay Following Birth

Your baby required care in the neonatal intensive care unit after birth.

Resuscitation at Birth

Your baby needed help to start breathing when they were born.

Seizures in the Newborn Period

Your baby had seizures in the days following birth.

Fetal Distress

There were concerns on the fetal monitor during labor.

Uterine Rupture

The uterus ruptured during labor or delivery.

Placental Abruption

The placenta separated from the uterus before the baby was born.

Cooling Therapy

Therapeutic hypothermia — a treatment used for babies who were deprived of oxygen during the labor process.
